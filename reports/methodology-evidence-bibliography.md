## Closest BTC/crypto empirical papers

1. Ahmed El Youssefi, Abdelaaziz Hessane, Imad Zeroual, Yousef Farhaoui (2025). “Optimizing Forecast Accuracy in Cryptocurrency Markets: Evaluating Feature Selection Techniques for Technical Indicators.” *Computers, Materials & Continua*, 83(2), 3411–3433. DOI: https://doi.org/10.32604/cmc.2025.063218. Full text: https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v83n2/60595/html. **Read depth:** full text Sections 1–6 and methods/results; figures extracted as image, exact numerical charts not audited. BTC/USDT, ETH/USDT, BNB/USDT; Binance minute data aggregated daily; 2017/2018–Jan 2025; 130+ technical indicators; MI, RFE, Random Forest Importance; K=20, horizons 1–20d; initial 2-year train, 6-month validation, one-month step; R²/RMSLE; states logarithmic returns target. Critical overlap: RQ asks temporal stability across horizons and effect on performance. Not shown in reviewed text to be successive-fold subset stability paired with corresponding OOS error. Results vary by model/horizon; no universal K or selector conclusion.
2. Barak & Parvini (2023). “Transfer-entropy-based dynamic feature selection for evaluating Bitcoin price drivers.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/fut.22453. **Read depth:** identity/title/abstract metadata only during this pass; publisher full text unavailable/was not verified. Bitcoin dynamic feature selection is adjacent; exact split/features and paired stability-error analysis UNCLEAR. Do not treat as proof either way.
3. Bysik & Ślepaczuk (2026). “Machine Learning-Based Bitcoin Trading Under Transaction Costs: Evidence From Walk-Forward Forecasting.” arXiv:2606.00060v1. https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060v1. **Read depth:** full HTML retrieved, methods/results/appendix partly inspected. BTC/USDT hourly, 27 walk-forward folds; Jaccard adjacent subset diagnostic in appendix; reported mean .673, range .333–1.000 per project’s prior extraction. No stability-error regression/correlation identified in reviewed text. Trading and hourly setup differs.
4. Ahmed? Elmakias, Kolsky & Vilenchik (2026). “Feature Stability as a Trust Layer for Feature Selection: Resampling-Based Recurrence Profiles Beyond Predictive Performance.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/math14132372. Publisher: https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/13/2372. **Read depth:** publisher full text/abstract extraction partial; stability profile / recurrence idea and non-BTC context verified. Not BTC temporal forecasting evidence.
5. Lazebnik & Rosenfeld (2024). “A new definition for feature selection stability analysis.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10472-024-09936-8. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10472-024-09936-8. **Read depth:** publisher record/abstract; full method not independently extracted in this pass. Generic methodological support only.
6. Océane Bel, Sinjoni Mukhopadhyay, Nathan Tallent, Faisal Nawab, Darrell Long (2021). “WinnowML: Stable feature selection for maximizing prediction accuracy of time-based system modeling.” *IEEE Big Data 2021*, pp. 3031–3041. PNNL record: https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/winnowml-stable-feature-selection-maximizing-prediction-accuracy-time-based-system; PNNL Datahub metadata: https://data.pnnl.gov/group/nodes/publication/32894. **Read depth:** official record/metadata; paper methods not read. The title/record supports time-based system modeling, not Bitcoin market forecasting. Claims about exact accuracy/stability gains need full text verification.

Additional source-status note: a parallel verification independently confirms DOI/authors and IEEE conference metadata through PNNL Datahub. It did not inspect full paper methods, so no performance numbers are admitted.

8. Peng et al. (2021). “Feature selection and deep neural networks for stock price direction forecasting using technical analysis indicators.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mlwa.2021.100060. **Read depth:** publisher record only in this pass; stock direction not BTC return forecasting.

## Time-series architecture and feature selection methods

9. Nie et al. (2023). “A Time Series is Worth 64 Words: Long-term Forecasting with Transformers” (PatchTST). arXiv:2211.14730. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730. **Read depth:** original paper retrieved, architecture and benchmark-level evidence; general long-horizon datasets, not direct BTC daily returns.
10. Liu et al. (2024). “iTransformer: Inverted Transformers Are Effective for Time Series Forecasting.” DOI/arXiv identity should be confirmed in a follow-up before citation. **Read depth:** not reviewed sufficiently; no methodology recommendation rests on it.
11. Nogueira, Sechidis & Brown (2018). “On the Stability of Feature Selection Algorithms.” *JMLR* 18(174), 1–54. https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/17-514.html. Generic feature selection stability, estimator perspective.
12. Kuncheva, L. I. (2007). “A Stability Index for Feature Selection.” https://lucykuncheva.co.uk/papers/lkAIA07.pdf. Kuncheva chance-corrected overlap; fixed equal subset cardinality and universe assumptions relevant.
13. Chen & Guestrin (2016). “XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/2939672.2939785. Algorithm source; does not establish Gain as best stability selector.

## Evaluation/inference method sources

14. Hyndman & Athanasopoulos. *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, forecast accuracy: https://otexts.com/fpptr/accuracy.html. MAE/RMSE definitions and interpretation; general method.
15. Tashman (2000). “Out-of-sample tests of forecasting accuracy: an analysis and review.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-2070(00)00065-0. Forecast evaluation protocol and rolling-origin context.
16. Künsch (1989). “The jackknife and the bootstrap for general stationary observations.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176347265. Dependent stationary observations.
17. Politis & Romano (1994). “The stationary bootstrap.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870. Random block-length bootstrap for dependent sequences; assumptions must be checked.
18. Diebold & Mariano (1995). “Comparing Predictive Accuracy.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599. Forecast-loss comparison framework; not automatically an inference method for stability-error association.
19. Andrews (1991). “Heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent covariance matrix estimation.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1991.10475156. HAC estimator, general serial dependence.
20. Chen & Guestrin XGBoost official API, `get_score` importance types. https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/python/python_api.html. Gain/Weight/Cover definition documented by API; implementation reference, not evidence of best selector.
21. SciPy `spearmanr` reference. https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.spearmanr.html. Rank association function and p-value calculation; p-value defaults do not handle overlapping-window dependence.
22. Liu, Zhang et al. (2024). “iTransformer: Inverted Transformers Are Effective for Time Series Forecasting.” arXiv:2310.06625. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625. Original architecture paper; general TS benchmarks, not direct BTC daily return evidence.

23. Dürre et al. (2023). “Significance testing of rank cross-correlations between autocorrelated time series with short-range dependence.” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/02664763.2022.2137115. More relevant than ordinary Spearman p-values when both series are autocorrelated; exact application to overlapping rolling-window metrics remains a methodological adaptation.


## Search coverage / limitations

Search was scoped to methodology choices plus user-required papers. Primary source priority followed where accessible. Distinct studies are not counted as “full read” unless methods/results actually inspected. Publisher extraction limits and full-text access gaps leave exact configuration of Barak & Parvini, Tripathi & Sharma, Peng, WinnowML and iTransformer unverified. This bibliography is an audit bibliography, not exhaustive systematic-review set.
